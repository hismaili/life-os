a domain model, not a template

Stop rebuilding your Notion setup every January.

LifeOS isn't a template you buy once and slowly break. It's the actual data model behind your projects, tasks, habits, knowledge, and journal — Notion just renders it. One CLI command provisions the whole workspace, relations already wired. Run it again next year and it reconciles instead of duplicating.

what usually happens

The template is beautiful for exactly one week.

You buy the $40 template, or you build your own from a YouTube video at 1am. It's gorgeous for about a week. Then you add a project that doesn't fit the existing Areas, or a habit that should roll up into a goal that doesn't exist in that database yet — and the relations start breaking in ways you don't understand well enough to fix without starting over.

So you rebuild it. Maybe in Notion again. Maybe you migrate to Obsidian, or Craft, or a paper notebook, because at least paper doesn't have a schema you can silently corrupt. Either way, the actual structure — how a habit connects to a goal, how a stray note becomes a project — gets thrown out and rebuilt from nothing. Again.

the actual difference

Notion is the view. This is the model.

Most productivity systems are their tool. LifeOS keeps the two separate on purpose: a real domain model — with actual invariants, actual relationships between Projects and Areas and Goals — that gets provisioned into Notion as one of its possible views. If you ever leave Notion, the model doesn't leave with it.

It's built on the methodologies you already half-remember from books you own but haven't finished: GTD for capturing and reviewing everything that lands on you, PARA for the Projects / Areas / Resources / Archive shape of things, a Zettelkasten for notes that link to each other instead of sitting in a folder, Atomic Habits' identity-based habit loop, OKRs for goals that get reviewed instead of written once and forgotten. Nothing reinvented — just finally wired into one system instead of five fighting each other.

what gets provisioned

One dashboard. Seven databases. Already related.

01 — projects

Projects

Tied to the Area it serves, so "why am I doing this" is one click away, not a question you ask yourself in a meeting.

02 — tasks

Tasks

The actual work, always pointing back to the project it belongs to — no orphaned to-dos floating in a database of their own.

03 — knowledge

Knowledge

Your Zettelkasten. Notes link to notes, instead of sitting in a folder waiting to be organized "someday."

04 — habits

Habits

Recurring actions tracked as a loop with a real cadence, not a checkbox you forget existed by week two.

05 — journal

Journal

Timestamped entries connected to whatever project or habit was actually happening that day — not a diary in isolation.

06 — resources

Resources

The links and docs you saved and were never going to find again. Now you will, because they're attached to what they're actually for.

07 — people

People

Half your projects are really about a person. That context shouldn't have to live only in your head.

how it actually runs

One command. Reconciled, not duplicated.

workspace create builds the whole thing — dashboard, all seven databases, the relations between them — under whatever Notion page you point it at. Run it again after adding a project by hand, and it notices what's already there and reconciles instead of cloning a second dashboard next to the first.

That's not a nice-to-have. That's what "idempotent" is supposed to mean, and it's the one thing no Notion template we've ever used has bothered to get right.

Built in the open. Not finished. That's the point right now.

The dashboard and all seven databases provision today. Relations, rollups, formulas, and sample-data seeding are still being wired in — and the orchestrator is honest about it: if a step isn't built yet, the report says FAILED, not a fake green checkmark. We'd rather you see exactly what's real than pretend the whole thing is done.

Built with Spring Boot, Java 21, and more YAML than anyone should have to write.
