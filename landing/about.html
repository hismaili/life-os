about lifeos

A model of your life, not a template of it.

LifeOS is a platform-agnostic domain model for a personal operating system. The model is the product — Notion is just one view rendered on top of it.

01 — the ideaThe tool keeps changing. The structure shouldn't.

Most productivity systems are their tool. The moment you outgrow the tool — or it raises its price, or you just want a change — the structure you carefully built goes with it. How a habit connects to a goal, how a stray note becomes a project, why a given task even exists: all of that lived only inside the app, and now it's gone.

LifeOS keeps the two apart on purpose. There's a real domain model underneath — with actual invariants and actual relationships between Projects, Areas, Goals, Tasks, Habits, Knowledge, Journal entries and People — and Notion is provisioned as one of its possible views. If you ever leave Notion, the model doesn't leave with it.

02 — what's insideOne dashboard. Seven databases. Already related.

A single command builds the whole workspace under a Notion page you choose — the dashboard, all seven databases, and the relations between them already wired:

  • Projects — tied to the Area they serve, so purpose is one click away.
  • Tasks — the actual work, always pointing back to its project.
  • Knowledge — a Zettelkasten: notes that link to notes, not folders.
  • Habits — recurring actions tracked as a loop with a real cadence.
  • Journal — timestamped entries connected to what was happening that day.
  • Resources — the links and docs you saved, attached to what they're for.
  • People — because half your projects are really about a person.

03 — the methodologiesNothing reinvented. Finally wired together.

The model encodes the methodologies you already half-remember from books you own but haven't finished: GTD for capturing and reviewing everything that lands on you, PARA for the Projects / Areas / Resources / Archive shape of things, a Zettelkasten for linked notes, Atomic Habits' identity-based habit loop, and OKRs for goals that get reviewed instead of written once and forgotten. Nothing invented — just stopped fighting each other across five different apps.

04 — idempotent by designRun it again. It reconciles, not duplicates.

workspace create is safe to run more than once. Add a project by hand, run it again, and it notices what's already there and reconciles instead of cloning a second dashboard next to the first. That's what "idempotent" is supposed to mean — and it's the one thing no Notion template we've used has bothered to get right.

05 — where it standsBuilt in the open. Not finished — honestly.

The dashboard and all seven databases provision today. Relations, rollups, formulas, and sample-data seeding are still being wired in — and the orchestrator is honest about it: if a step isn't built yet, the report says FAILED, not a fake green checkmark. It's a real, early-stage project built in public, not a finished product with a waiting list.

the short version

LifeOS is a domain model for your life, provisioned into Notion by one idempotent command. The structure survives the tool. It's open source under the MIT license and a work in progress.
